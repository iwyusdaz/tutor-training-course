# Usability test script (materials pilot)

Status: **prepared, not run.** Used with the [pilot protocol](pilot-protocol.md); consent first ([consent text](consent-text.md)).

## Facilitator preparation (day before)

1. Confirm the participant has consented and has a laptop/keyboard.
2. Open a fresh browser profile: the site at `http://127.0.0.1:4000` (local build) or the published URL; no extensions, no autocomplete history.
3. Print: pre-task sheet (set A), post-task sheet (set B), this script, a notes grid.
4. Decide the recording policy: **typed notes only** (no audio, no screen recording) unless the participant separately opted in on the consent form.
5. Write the participant code (P1…P5) on everything — no names.

## Script

**Introduction (2 min, read as written):**

> "Thanks for doing this. You're helping test training materials for new math tutors — two modules and a short practice activity. There are no right answers to any of this: wherever it's confusing, that's a finding, not a mistake you made. I'll ask you to think out loud where you're comfortable; silence is fine too. I'm not testing you and I'm not helping — if you get stuck, that's exactly what I need to see, so I'll stay quiet and take notes. Any questions before we start?"

**Pre-task (7 min):**

> "First, three short written scenarios. Answer each in a sentence or two, as you would to a real student. Don't worry about being fancy — write what you'd actually say."

Hand the set-A sheet. Do not answer clarifying questions about the task beyond "answer as you would to the student." Note (grid, timestamps): hesitation, re-reads, questions asked, phrasing chosen.

**Modules (23 min):**

> "Now the two modules, in order, at your own pace. Talk me through what you're thinking as you read — what's clear, what's odd, what you'd skip."

Stay silent unless asked a direct question; answer only with "what do you think it means?" Note (grid): every scroll-back, every wrong turn, every "huh?", time per module, any section skipped, anything read aloud.

**Practice tool (10 min):**

> "Last activity — a short practice tool in the browser. It's not a test; wrong answers are set up to be the useful ones. Use it however you like — mouse or keyboard."

Note: input mode chosen (keyboard vs mouse), any focus loss or confusion, whether feedback was read, whether they changed answers on later items, anything the tool didn't let them do.

**Post-task (7 min):** same script as pre-task; set B. **No hints from anything they just read may be voiced by the facilitator** — if the participant asks "was that right?", the answer is "that's what we score afterwards."

**Interview (10 min):** the three protocol questions, then: "Was anything inaccessible or annoying — contrast, focus, keyboard, reading order?"

**Close (2 min):** "Thank you. Your responses get scored blind against the rubric, and the report on the site will only show anonymized quotes. If you want to withdraw, email me by [date] and your sheets are shredded unread." Hand them the thank-you note.

## Notes grid (one per participant, per step)

| Step | Timestamp | What they did | What it might mean (write later, not live) |
|---|---|---|---|

Rule: **behavior first, interpretation later** — the grid's third column is filled after the session, not during, to keep observations from being contaminated by the facilitator's guesses.

## Debrief (facilitator, same day)

1. Sticking points per participant, ranked.
2. Any behavior that indicates a *materials* fix vs a *participant* quirk.
3. Anything the [blind scoring](blind-scoring-sheet.md) later contradicts.
4. Draft revision list → goes to the report and the changelog with the pilot citation.
