# Calibration activity

{: .label .label-blue }
90 minutes, before first observations, then twice per semester · For site coordinators and anyone who scores tutors with the [rubric](observation-rubric.md)

## Why calibrate

The [measurement plan](../program/measurement-plan.md) tracks rubric averages and growth (+0.5 between observations 1 and 3) — numbers that only mean something if the people producing them score the same session the same way. Inter-rater drift is the quiet killer of observation systems: one generous scorer and one severe scorer turn a program average into noise. Calibration is the fix, and it is cheap: score the same fictional transcript, compare, argue the gaps, converge.

## The activity (90 minutes)

**Materials:** the [observation rubric](observation-rubric.md), printed; three calibration transcripts — **[T1](../transcripts/t1.md), [T3](../transcripts/t3.md), [T5](../transcripts/t5.md)** — chosen because they anchor different ends and mixtures (T1 ≈ strong across domains; T3 = mixed, split scoring; T5 = the weak end); independent scoring sheets.

**Round 1 · Independent scoring (30 min).** Everyone scores T1 silently, alone: five domain scores + one evidence line each. No talking. Collect the sheets *before* any discussion — comparison needs independent judgments, not agreed ones.

**Round 2 · Compare (20 min).** Post all scores for T1. For each domain: range? If Domain 3 ranges 2–4, the discussion is the activity:

> Scorer A: "I gave a 4 — every problem opened with a question."
> Scorer B: "I gave a 3 — she explained first on the second problem."
> Facilitator: "Read the level-4 anchor out loud. 'Every problem opens with a probing question.' Is 'every' met?"
> Scorer A: "…no. Three of five. That's a 3."

The transcript annotations (numbered after each exchange) are the tiebreaker: point at the tick that settles it. **Anchors beat seniority** — the most experienced voice doesn't win; the anchor text does.

**Round 3 · The hard one (20 min).** Score T5 (weak end). The drift that matters most is *severity*: new scorers round up for warmth ("she was so prepared!"). Anchor discipline: the rubric scores **student production**, not tutor effort. If Domain 3 comes out ≥2 for T5, re-read annotation 1: ninety seconds, zero questions, students produce nothing. The correct score is 1 — feel the difference; that's the calibration.

**Round 4 · Split scoring (20 min).** T3 exists to prove the rubric scores domains separately: questioning at 4, data use at 1, in the *same session*. Anyone who "averages T3 to a 3" misses the design — and would coach T3's tutor wrong (the [T3 debrief](../transcripts/t3.md) leads with the strength precisely because the weakness is in a different domain). *Note: the transcripts' own "Rubric read" sections were re-scored against the anchors on 2026-10-26 (T3's Domain 2 = 1 per the exit-ticket cap, Domain 1 = 3; T6's Domain 2 = 1; T4's Domain 5 = 2), so the published scores and this protocol now agree — if you and a page ever disagree again, the anchor text wins.*

**Close (a few minutes):** record the agreed scores in the [calibration log](#calibration-log) and set the year's rules of the house (below).

## Rules of the house (established in round 2, kept for the year)

1. **Lower level wins when between levels** — unless the higher behavior is clearly present (rubric rule; calibration makes it habit).
2. **Count, don't vibe.** "Wait time" is 3–5 *counted* seconds; "specific feedback" names *the work*. If you can't point at the tick, you can't score it.
3. **Warmth is not a score.** Prepared, kind tutors can score 1–2; sessions are scored on student production ([T5] is the standing reminder).
4. **Score domains separately, always.** No overall averages in the debrief.
5. **The annotations settle disputes** — in calibration and in real debriefs, point at the exchange, not at your experience.
