# Coaching conversation guide

{: .label .label-blue }
For the 15-minute debrief inside the [coaching cycle](coaching-cycle.md) · Pairs with the [observation rubric](observation-rubric.md) (evidence) and the tutor's [self-reflection](/tutor-training-course/tools/tutor-self-reflection.md) (their words first)

{: .note }
**Alignment note:** the conversation structure below — evidence first, one strength, one focus, tutor generates the how — deliberately mirrors the feedback approach in the companion portfolio project [Should this be a course?](https://iwyusdaz.github.io/should-this-be-a-course/) (repo 02), which builds a manager feedback model from the same evidence-first principle. Where that project's job aid gives managers five minutes, this guide gives coordinators fifteen and a rubric behind every sentence.

---

## 0 · Before the debrief (2 minutes of prep)

Re-read the [rubric sheet](observation-rubric.md) ticks, the tutor's self-reflection, and any [tracker flags](/tutor-training-course/tools/student-progress-tracker.md). Write down: (a) your strength + the tick that proves it; (b) your single focus move + the rubric line; (c) your micro-practice plan. Walk in with three things written; improvise nothing.

## 1 · Open with their week, not your form (1 minute)

> "How did the group feel this week, from your side?"

Then *listen for the same thing your rubric saw*. A tutor who says "the tickets kept slipping" has already started the data conversation for you; a tutor who says "great week!" gets your evidence sooner. Their reflection — if they shared it — is your opening line.

**Why:** the debrief is a coaching conversation, not an audit reading. The first minute decides which one it becomes.

## 2 · Name the strength with evidence (3 minutes)

Open with what you *saw*, tick-anchored:

> "Three times today you asked before you explained — the distributive one was textbook: 'What did the 2 multiply?' and Amara found −8 herself. That's the strongest questioning I've seen from you."

Rules: evidence before adjectives; name the *behavior* (they can repeat it), not the trait ("you're a natural" teaches nothing); tie to growth where true ("last observation you asked once; today, three times").

**Why first:** a debrief that opens with the fix teaches tutors to dread observations. A debrief that opens with *evidenced* strength teaches them the rubric is a lens, not a trap — and it keeps the strength on the record when the focus move comes.

## 3 · Land one focus move (5 minutes)

**Name the move, show the evidence, shrink the gap:**

> "I want to work on one thing: wait time. I counted three times you asked a good question and then answered it yourself inside two seconds — the x-on-both-sides one, at about minute twelve. The students never got the silence. Here's what I'd love: three seconds of nothing after every question. Count it. That's the whole move."

Rules:
- **One. Move.** Not "wait time and also your exit tickets and also—" One.
- **Evidence, then the move as the gap-bridger.** "Three times you self-answered" is evidence; "be patient" is a virtue. The rubric line is the bridge.
- **The tutor proposes the how** ("What would make three seconds possible mid-sentence?"). Self-generated tactics stick; assigned tactics decay. If they stall, offer the two that work: the silent count on the plan sheet, or writing the question down in advance ([Module 3](/tutor-training-course/modules/3-questions-wait-time-and-errors.md)).

## 4 · Micro-practice the move (3 minutes)

> "Show me. I'm Amara — I wrote 2x − 4 for 2(x − 4). Go."

Two minutes, right there: tutor runs the move, coach plays a realistic student, immediate feedback ("you held five seconds — did you feel it?"). If the move is structural (exit-ticket protection), rehearse the *sentence*: "Two minutes left — land this one, tickets out."

**Why in the debrief:** a focus move not rehearsed is a New Year's resolution. Two minutes now buys the transfer; the [coaching cycle](coaching-cycle.md) makes it a fixed step for this reason.

## 5 · Close with the trail (2 minutes)

> "So: focus move is the three-second pause. I'll look for it — explicitly — when I observe you in two weeks. What will leave a trail I can see?"
> *(Good answers: pauses ticked on the plan sheet; the question written in advance; "you'll hear the silence.")*

Name the re-observation date, confirm what you'll both ignore for now (important — say it out loud: "your review block is fine; I'm not watching it this cycle"), and end on the strength.

## When tutors get defensive — three scripts

**"You only see 30 minutes. I have bad days."**
> "Absolutely true — one observation is one slice. That's why we re-look every two or three weeks and why growth is measured across three observations, not one. What I can promise: what I write down is only what I saw today, with the minute it happened."
*(Concede the true part; re-anchor to the cycle's design. Never argue the slice by generalizing from it — "well, everyone says…" is the road to a rubric nobody trusts.)*

**"That student would've kept going if you'd let me explain."**
> "Maybe — and it's worth testing, which is exactly why the move is 'wait three seconds' and not 'never explain.' Run it as an experiment for two weeks: your plan sheet, the pause, and we look at what happened to the students' next tickets together."
*(Convert verdicts into experiments. The rubric's authority comes from being testable, and so should the coach's.)*

**"This is the third focus move in two months. I'm drowning."**
> "That's real, and it's on me — if three things are 'focused,' nothing is. Let's pick ONE and park the others in writing: your review block is fine, your data loop is fine, I'm not watching them this cycle. Which of the two on the table do you want first?"
*(Over-focus is a coach error, not a tutor flaw. Fix it visibly — name what's parked — or trust dies.)*

## What this guide deliberately omits

No "compliment sandwich," no "two stars and a wish," no written-up scores in the meeting (scores live on the [rubric sheet](observation-rubric.md) and in the [measurement plan](/tutor-training-course/program/measurement-plan.md); the conversation is about moves). The structure is: their week → evidence-anchored strength → one focus move → rehearse it → name the trail. Everything else is ceremony, and ceremony is what makes 15 minutes too short.
