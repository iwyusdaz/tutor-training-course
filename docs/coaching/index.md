# Coaching system

Coaching is how training survives contact with real sessions. This section gives the fictional program's site coordinators four connected tools:

1. **[Observation rubric](observation-rubric.md)** — five domains × four levels with behavioral anchors; one page; usable inside a 30-minute observation. Its domains are the [competency model](/tutor-training-course/research/competency-model.md)'s domains, word for word — what is trained is what is observed.
2. **[Coaching cycle](coaching-cycle.md)** — observe → 15-minute debrief within 48 hours → one strength + one focus move → micro-practice → re-observe, every 2–3 weeks. Rendered as a diagram and as text.
3. **[Coaching conversation guide](coaching-conversation-guide.md)** — openings, naming strengths with evidence, choosing the single highest-leverage focus, and scripts for defensiveness. Aligned with the feedback model in the companion project [Should this be a course?](https://iwyusdaz.github.io/should-this-be-a-course/) (a performance-consulting case study whose conversation approach this guide deliberately mirrors).
4. **[Calibration activity](calibration-activity.md)** — coordinators score the same transcript and compare, before their scores are ever trusted to track tutor growth ([measurement plan](/tutor-training-course/program/measurement-plan.md) requires calibration evidence).

{: .note }
**Why rubric + cycle + conversation and not a "quality checklist":** a checklist tells a tutor *whether* they passed. A rubric with behavioral anchors tells them *what to do differently*, the cycle makes that happen on a schedule, and the calibration makes the scores mean something. All three are observable-design choices, not best-practice gestures — each traces to the [competency model](/tutor-training-course/research/competency-model.md) and to a metric in the [measurement plan](/tutor-training-course/program/measurement-plan.md).
