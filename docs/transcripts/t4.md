# Transcript T4 · Structure intact, feedback generic

{: .label .label-yellow }
Mixed session · Tutor: Marcus (community member, week 10) · Students: Priya, Camila, Andre (Grade 8) · Skill: solving `ax + b = c`; misconception in play: equals sign as "the answer comes next" (M5)

{: .note }
**Fictional transcript, constructed for training purposes.** All names and exchanges are invented; no real children are depicted. Numbered annotations follow each exchange — [format](/tutor-training-course/transcripts/).

---

**[0:00]**

**Marcus:** Priya, Camila, Andre — sit, sit. Warm-up's up: `2x + 6 = 14` and a stretch problem if you're fast. Four minutes, like always.

> **[1] S1 (routine running: warm-up launched at minute zero — the habit T3 lacked).** But notice what's missing: no names-with-reference, no connection to yesterday's ticket. The structure is *in place*; the relationship and data layers are running on autopilot.

**[0:05] Review**

**Marcus:** Yesterday's ticket — most of you got the last one, good. The one people missed was the `= 7` one. Answer was 2. Any questions on that? No? Moving on.

> **[2] D1/S1 (the review block *exists* and uses ticket data — but see [3]: it delivers answers, not diagnosis).** "Most of you got it" is an average doing an individual's job.

**Andre:** Yeah, I didn't get that one. I had `x + 4 = 7` and put 7 after the x.

**Marcus:** Okay, noted — we'll hit that later.

> **[3] Missed moment: Andre has just handed the tutor a written confession of the M5 misconception (equals sign as "answer comes next": he reads `x + 4 = 7` left-to-right and treats `=` as "here comes the answer slot").** The strong move is a probing question *now*: "When you wrote 7 after the x, what were you saying the whole line meant?" Marcus defers it to "later" — and "later" never arrives.

**[0:08] Guided practice**

**Marcus:** Board: `3x + 5 = 20`. Priya, take us.

**Priya:** Subtract 5 both sides: `3x = 15`. Divide by 3: x = 5.

**Marcus:** Good job! Camila, next one: `4x − 2 = 18`.

**Camila:** Add 2 both sides: `4x = 20`… divide: x = 5!

**Marcus:** Nice work!

> **[4] F1 (the session's core weakness, visible in two lines: "Good job!" and "Nice work!" — praise with zero content, interchangeable between students and problems).** Neither student hears *what* she did right (subtract 5 from both sides *kept the balance* / moved the constant before the coefficient). The rubric's Domain 4 anchor for level 2 is nearly verbatim this: "praise is generic; the student could not say what to repeat."

**Andre:** Can I go? `6x = 24` — that's just x = 4.

**Marcus:** Good job, Andre.

> **[5] R4 pressure building: Andre is quick and starts collecting the room's praise.** Watch [8] — nothing here redirects him *into the work* (the strong move is a "prove it" challenge), so his speed becomes the session's currency.

**[0:16] Guided practice, continued**

**Marcus:** Harder one: `5x − 3 = 2x + 9`. Priya?

**Priya:** I got x = 4.

**Marcus:** *(beat)* How'd you get there?

**Priya:** I… subtracted the 2x and added the 3? It came out to x = 4.

**Marcus:** *(two seconds, then)* It's actually x = 4? Let me see — okay: `5x − 2x = 3x`, and `9 + 3 = 12`, so `3x = 12`, x = 4. Yes, you're right!

> **[6] Mixed moment #1: the tutor re-derives the solution at the board, filling the pause — wait time [Q4] never happens; the student's vague "it came out to" stays vague.** The fix landed correctly *in math* (Priya's answer was right) but the *why* is now the tutor's, not hers. The level-4 version: three seconds of silence, then "Walk me through the subtract-2x step — what did the right side become?"

**Camila:** Wait, how did the 3 get to the other side? I got x = 2.

**Marcus:** Camila, you subtract… hold on. Priya, help her out?

**Priya:** You move the −3 over and it becomes plus 3, so…

> **[7] Mixed moment #2: peer tutoring offered instead of a probing question — and Priya explains with the memorized "move it over and flip" procedure, the exact surface rule that produces M1/M2 errors later.** The strong move: "Camila, when you moved the −3, what operation did you perform on *both* sides?" Marcus defers to the quick student instead of the [hint ladder](https://github.com/iwyusdaz/socratic-ai-tutor/blob/main/spec/hint-ladder.md) — faster, and it teaches less.

**[0:25] Independent try + exit ticket**

**Marcus:** Solo problem, three minutes: `7x + 2 = 23`. Then ticket's on the table — same skill, don't rush.

*(Tickets given, collected.)*

**Marcus:** Andre, since you're quick — start the stretch problems. Priya, Camila, same time tomorrow.

> **[8] S3 + D1 (the ticket happens — full credit on the loop's *mechanics*) + R4 (Andre's speed gets more work, not a role in the room).** Note the contrast with T3: Marcus *keeps* the data habit and *loses* the thinking; T3 kept the thinking and lost the habit. Add the two and you get [T1](t1.md) — which is exactly how the rubric is built: five separate domains, not one "good/bad" grade.

## What to notice in T4

- **Domain 2 is solid** — blocks run on time, review happens from ticket data, exit ticket given and collected [1][2][8].
- **Domain 4 fails quietly**: generic praise [4], a why-question never asked, the tutor re-deriving at [6]. Nobody in the room can say *what* they did right.
- **The M5 misconception walked in the door at [3] and was never picked up** — Andre's "I put 7 after the x" is a textbook equals-sign-as-answer-slot disclosure, and the session was too busy to notice. The [exit-ticket bank](/tutor-training-course/tools/exit-ticket-bank.md) tags M5 items precisely so this doesn't depend on the tutor catching it live.
- Andre is quick, engaged — and being shaped into "the fast one" [5][8] rather than given real responsibility ("prove it to the group").

## Rubric read

[Observation rubric](/tutor-training-course/coaching/observation-rubric.md): Domain 1 = 2 (names, but no references to recorded work) · Domain 2 = 4 · Domain 3 = 2 (no probing questions on errors; tutor re-derives at [6]) · Domain 4 = 2 (generic praise throughout; no confirm-and-ask-why) · Domain 5 = 3 (ticket given and collected; no evidence results shaped the next plan). T4 is the calibration anchor for **Domain 4, level 2**.
