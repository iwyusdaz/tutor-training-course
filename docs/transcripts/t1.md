# Transcript T1 · A strong session

{: .label .label-blue }
Strong session · Tutor: Nina (college junior, week 6) · Students: Amara, Jayden, Luis (Grade 7) · Skill: two-step equations; review: distributing incorrectly

{: .note }
**Fictional transcript, constructed for training purposes.** These are not recordings of real children; names and sessions are invented. Annotations are numbered after each exchange — [why this format](index.md). Move names follow the shared [tutoring moves vocabulary](https://github.com/iwyusdaz/socratic-ai-tutor/blob/main/spec/pedagogy-rules.md).

**[0:00]**

**Nina:** Welcome back — Amara, Jayden, Luis, same seats. Amara, yesterday your distributive step finally clicked; that's where we start today. Luis, last week you told me negative numbers were "out to get you," and then your exit ticket disagreed — so I'm watching you today.

> **[1] R1 + R2 (greeting by name; specific reference to recorded work).** All three students individually greeted inside 30 seconds. The Luis line uses his own joke — teasing that builds, tied to *evidence from the tracker* (R3), not mood.

**Amara:** The one with the parentheses?

**Nina:** That's the one. Warm-up's on the board — three problems like yesterday's exit ticket, solo start, two minutes. Timer's running.

> **[2] S1 (warm-up within 2 minutes of start; the block reactivates last session's skill — the same skill the exit ticket tagged).** Solo start = R4: everyone has a job; no one waits for the group.

**[0:05] Warm-up check**

**Nina:** Pens down. Quick hands: thumbs on the last one — `3(x + 2) − 4`. Did the 3 hit both terms? [thumbs up around the table] Good. That's review done; ticket said "distribute," and you distributed.

> **[3] S1 review block + D3 (review topic chosen from exit-ticket results — and said out loud, so students see the data loop working).** Public proof that tickets change plans: "ticket said, so we did."

**[0:10] Guided practice**

**Nina:** New problem, on the board: `2(x − 4) + 3 = 11`. Before I say anything — Amara, what do you notice?

> **[4] Q1 (probing question, L0 — attempt/observation before explanation).** Nina's first move is handing the problem to a student, not a mini-lecture.

**Amara:** There's parentheses *and* a plus 3.

**Nina:** True. So what's your first step, and why that one first?

> **[5] Q1 again — "why that one first" asks the student to expose her reasoning, not just act.**

**Amara:** Distribute first? Because… the parentheses are in the way?

**Nina:** You've got a reason and it's a good one — the 2 multiplies *everything* inside. Show us.

> **[6] F1 (specific feedback — names what was right in her reasoning, then hands her the pen).** Note it is not "good job": it confirms *her stated reason*.

**Amara:** So… 2x minus 8 plus 3.

**Nina:** Stop there — Jayden, what do you notice about what Amara just did?

> **[7] Q1 + R4 (spread attention: Amara produced, Jayden verifies — the strongest student gets a real job, not the answers).**

**Jayden:** She did minus 8. Not minus 4. Because 2 × −4.

**Nina:** Exactly the trap this problem sets, and she dodged it. Luis, what's next?

> **[8] F1 + F2 (specific + normalizing: "the trap this problem sets" frames the error as the problem's trick, not Amara's past failure).**

**Luis:** Plus 3… wait, minus 8 plus 3 is minus 5. So `2x − 5 = 11`.

**Nina:** *(silent, counting — four seconds)*

> **[9] Q4 (wait time 3–5 s).** The next step is Luis's to say. Silence is the tutor resisting the pull to confirm or prompt early.

**Luis:** …plus 5 both sides? So `2x = 16`.

**Nina:** Say why plus.

**Luis:** To move the −5… and the sign flips because we're subtracting minus 5.

**Nina:** You kept both sides balanced — that's the move. Now x?

> **[10] F3 (confirm and ask why — one why-question, brief confirm).** "You kept both sides balanced" names the *process*; the why-question checks he can own it. Next turn she moves on — no extra interrogation.

**Luis:** Divide by 2 — `x = 8`!

**Nina:** Check it for us.

**Luis:** 2(8 − 4) + 3… 8 + 3 = 11. Yes!

> **[11] Substitution check modeled by the *student* — accuracy belongs to the room, not the answer key.**

**[0:24] Independent try**

**Nina:** Same shape, new numbers: `3(x + 2) − 5 = 16`. Solo, four minutes — I'm not talking. If you finish, check by substituting.

> **[12] F4 (offer a similar problem — one small variation: coefficient 3, constant −5) + S1 (protected independent block, tutor silent).** "I'm not talking" is said warmly and is the point of the block.

**[0:29] Exit ticket**

**Nina:** Tickets out — one question, same family: `4(x − 1) + 2 = 14`. Two minutes, hand them in even if you're mid-thought; I read these tonight.

> **[13] S3 + D1 + D2 (exit ticket every session; collected even if incomplete; recorded same day — the data loop that [Module 5](../modules/5-using-exit-tickets-and-data.md) trains).** "I read these tonight" makes the loop visible to students, which is itself motivating: their work changes the plan.

## What to steal from T1

- The first 3 minutes: names + one specific recorded reference per student (Domain 1).
- The ladder discipline: at most one hint per turn; the tutor's longest turn is ~15 words.
- Wait time as a *counted* pause — annotation [9] — and "Say why" after correct steps.
- The exit ticket chosen *before* the session and connected out loud to today's review.
