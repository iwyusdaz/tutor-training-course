# Annotated transcripts

{: .note }
**All six transcripts are fictional and constructed for training purposes.** They are not recordings of real children; tutor and student names are invented. They are built from documented middle-school algebra misconceptions so that the errors students make in them are the errors real tutors will meet — the misconception research behind that choice is cited in the [evidence notes](/tutor-training-course/research/evidence-notes.md). A structured realism review (agent-based; practitioner review pending) checked each transcript against that research — see [evaluation methods](/tutor-training-course/evaluation/methods.md).

## Why transcripts instead of videos

Videos of real sessions would show real children — a privacy problem, and a consent problem minors can't meaningfully opt out of. Written fictional transcripts are private by construction, searchable, annotatable, and screen-reader accessible. They are also *editable*: trainers can adapt a transcript to their local curriculum without re-filming.

## The annotation format

Each transcript is a dialogue between the tutor and 2–4 students, broken into exchanges. **Numbered annotations follow each exchange** — never floating in a margin — and name the move the tutor used (e.g., *probing question*, *wait time*, *confirm and ask why*), which [competency domain](/tutor-training-course/research/competency-model.md) it serves, and what an observer would look for. Move names and hint-ladder levels (0–5) are shared with the companion [Socratic AI Tutor](https://iwyusdaz.github.io/socratic-ai-tutor/) project — same moves, encoded for an AI.

## The library

| # | Transcript | Verdict | The one thing it teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| [T1](t1.md) | Routine holding together | Strong | The first 3 minutes + a closed data loop |
| [T2](t2.md) | A student stuck in the same spot | Strong | Worked example after repeated failure (L4), done right |
| [T3](t3.md) | Questioning strong, data loop broken | Mixed | Good teaching ≠ a good program: the ticket matters |
| [T4](t4.md) | Structure intact, feedback generic | Mixed | "Good job" ×20 teaches nothing; the M5 door opens and closes |
| [T5](t5.md) | The tutor explains everything | Weak | Over-explaining: prepared, warm — and the students never think |
| [T6](t6.md) | The tutor answers for the students | Weak | No wait time, ladder skipped, speed as the prize |

**Suggested sequence for new tutors:** T1 → T5 → T2 → T6 → then [spot-the-move practice](practice/index.html), then T3 + T4 when your first observation is scheduled (they show why the rubric scores domains separately).

## Using transcripts in training

- **Spot-the-move (interactive):** [practice tool](practice/index.html) — 10 exchanges from this library; you name the move and whether it worked; feedback explains why. Nothing you answer is stored.
- **Live sessions:** [Session A](/tutor-training-course/live-practice/session-a.md) and [Session B](/tutor-training-course/live-practice/session-b.md) facilitator guides use T1/T2/T5/T6 excerpts as role-play material.
- **Calibration:** coordinators score T1, T3 and T5 against the [observation rubric](/tutor-training-course/coaching/observation-rubric.md) and compare scores — the [calibration activity](/tutor-training-course/coaching/calibration.md) protocol.
