# Transcript T3 · Questioning strong, data loop broken

{: .label .label-yellow }
Mixed session · Tutor: Diego (college sophomore, week 4) · Students: Jayla, Omar, Sofia (Grade 7) · Skill: two-step equations; misconception targeted: dividing only some terms (M4)

{: .note }
**Fictional transcript, constructed for training purposes.** Invented tutor and students; not a recording of real children. Numbered annotations follow each exchange — [format](/tutor-training-course/transcripts/).

---

**[0:00]**

**Diego:** Hey — Jayla, Omar, Sofia. Sofia, yesterday you nailed the check-back step; we're building on that. Jayla, I owe you a rematch on the puzzle from Tuesday. Omar — good to see you, man.

> **[1] R1 + R2 (strong open: every student named; two specific references).** Diego's relationships work is genuinely good — worth saying, because weak sessions are usually weak *somewhere specific*, not everywhere.

**[0:03] Warm-up — and where the session starts drifting**

**Diego:** Warm-up: `6x − 9 = 3x` on the board. Go.

*(Students work. At 0:08, Omar is still on his first step.)*

> **[2] S1 (weakness #1: warm-up has no review block — the previous exit ticket's common error is never surfaced to the group; no timer, so drift sets in).** The block still has a purpose (reactivate), but nothing ties it to recorded data (D3). The review block silently vanishes.

**[0:10] Guided practice**

**Diego:** Main event: `5x + 7 = 2x + 19`. Sofia, talk to me — what's your move?

**Sofia:** Get the x's together? Subtract 2x from both sides: `3x + 7 = 19`.

**Diego:** Clean. Why 2x and not the 7 first?

**Sofia:** Either works, but x's first means smaller numbers later.

> **[3] Q1 + F3 (probing question; "why" asked after a correct step).** Diego's questioning here is genuinely strong — he asks for reasoning, not just answers.

**Jayla:** I did `3x = 19 − 7`… so `3x = 12`… *(counts on fingers)* x = 4.

**Diego:** *(three seconds — real wait time)* And check it?

**Jayla:** 3(4) + 7 = 19. And 2(4) + 19 = 27… that's not equal?

> **[4] Q4 (wait time — good) but then the stumble: Jayla's *check* reveals the error, and the next move decides the session's quality.** Watch what Diego does with it.

**Diego:** Right, so where's the mismatch — left side or right?

**Jayla:** The right side. I moved the 19 over but… oh. I subtracted 2x on the left but not the right?

**Diego:** There it is. What does the equals sign promise?

**Jayla:** Both sides stay equal. So I needed `2x` gone from both: `3x + 7 = 19 − … no —` right side becomes just 19? No wait, minus nothing…

> **[5] Q3 (excellent error handling: the probing question exposes *where* the operation was applied to one side only — M4-flavored, via balance).** But see annotation [6]: the repair is now oscillating, and Diego is about to do the thing this program trains against — *not yet*, though. He almost holds.

**Diego:** Slow down. You had `2x + 19`. Subtracting `2x` from the right side leaves…?

**Jayla:** …19. Just 19.

**Diego:** And the left side?

**Jayla:** `3x + 7`. So `3x + 7 = 19`. Then x = 4 — *again*? But the check said no!

> **[6] The confusable moment: two errors stacked (the M4 one-side divide AND an arithmetic slip in the check).** Diego handles the *first* well. The second — the check arithmetic — he's about to start computing *for* her. Watch [7].

**Diego:** Recompute the check with x = 4: left is 3(4) + 7 = 19. Right is 2(4) + 19…

**Jayla:** 27.

**Diego:** So x = 4 fails the check. What does that tell you?

**Jayla:** …my solve was wrong somewhere?

> **[7] Mixed moment #1: the tutor does the arithmetic *for* the student ("left is 3(4)+7=19") instead of asking her to compute it.** Small, and it keeps the thinking alive — but it's the exact "answering for the student" habit [T6](t6.md) will take to its extreme. The stronger move: "Compute both sides with x = 4 — what do you get?"

**Diego:** Somewhere in the move. One more look at `5x + 7 = 2x + 19`: subtract 2x from both sides gives you…?

**Jayla:** `3x + 7 = 19`. That's what I had!

**Diego:** It is. So the solve was right — where else could the check have gone wrong?

**Jayla:** *(pause)* …2 times 4 is 8, plus 19… wait, the ORIGINAL right side is `2x + 19`. But I wrote the answer for x on my check wrong. I checked `2x + 19` where x was 4… oh no, 8 + 19 is 27, that IS the right side after I moved stuff. The original equation is `5x + 7 = 2x + 19` — 27 = 27!

**Diego:** There it is — your *solve* was right; your check mixed up which equation it was testing. That's why the check exists.

> **[8] F1 (when it lands, it's specific and correct: names exactly what was and wasn't wrong — the solve stood, the check was misapplied).** This is a genuinely good teach: Diego rescued a correct solution from a false alarm, and Jayla did the arithmetic herself on the recovery. Score Domain 3 at 4 here — and see [10] for what follows.

**[0:22] Independent try — cut short**

**Diego:** One solo problem: `4x + 3 = x + 12`. Three minutes.

*(At 0:26, Omar finishes; Diego starts walking through the solution at the board "since we have two minutes.")*

> **[9] Mixed moment #2: the independent try exists but is foreshortened, and the "walk-through at the board" restores the tutor as the producer of steps — undoing, at the end of the session, exactly the discipline the middle built.** Not fatal (Omar had finished; Jayla rechecks her work) — but the block's purpose (prove transfer without you) is compromised.

**[0:29] — and then the bell**

**Diego:** That's time — great session, everyone. Tickets tomorrow, I promise, we're out of minutes. Omar, stick around for one sec?

> **[10] The session's real failure: **no exit ticket** (S3, D1 broken).** "Tomorrow, I promise" is how the data loop dies politely — and [the measurement plan](/tutor-training-course/program/measurement-plan.md) tracks exactly this: sessions-with-exit-ticket is a headline fidelity metric, target ≥90%. Without the ticket, Diego can't choose Thursday's review from data (D3) and can't catch a student stuck two sessions running (D4). One skipped block, four downstream failures.

## What to notice in T3

- **Domain 3 is excellent** — probing questions, wait time [4], error exposed not corrected [5], the rescue at [8]. If the observation stopped at questioning, this session would look like a 4.
- **Domain 2 and Domain 5 fail quietly**: no review block [2], a compressed independent try [9], no exit ticket [10].
- This is the coaching conversation T3 is built for: the rubric's per-domain scoring *separates* the compliment from the fix, so the debrief leads with the strength (questioning) and lands on one focus (the ticket), not a lecture. See the [coaching conversation guide](/tutor-training-course/coaching/coaching-conversation-guide.md).

## Rubric read

[Observation rubric](/tutor-training-course/coaching/observation-rubric.md): Domain 1 = 4 · Domain 2 = 2 (no review block; independent try compromised; **no exit ticket**) · Domain 3 = 4 · Domain 4 = 3 (specific when it landed [8]; one instance of computing for the student [7]) · Domain 5 = 1 (no ticket given, collected, or recorded). T3 is a calibration anchor for **split scoring** — sessions are not overall "good" or "bad," and the rubric should say so per domain.
