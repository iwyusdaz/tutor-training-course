# Transcript T2 · A student stuck in the same spot

{: .label .label-blue }
Strong session · Tutor: Grace (career changer, former accountant, week 8) · Students: Mei, DeShawn, Elena, Tomás (Grade 8) · Skill: equations with variables on both sides; recurring misconception: sign errors when moving terms (M2)

{: .note }
**Fictional transcript, constructed for training purposes.** Names and sessions are invented; this is not a recording of real children. Numbered annotations follow each exchange — [format explained here](index.md).

---

**[0:00]**

**Grace:** Mei, DeShawn, Elena, Tomás — welcome back. Mei, your ticket yesterday had the cleanest "collect the x's" work I've seen; today you get first shot. Tomás, yesterday that minus-four problem — you and I aren't done with it, and that's fine: today's the day it surrenders.

> **[1] R1 + R2 + F2 (names; specific references from the tracker; struggle framed as "not done with it yet" — normalizing without shaming).** Note Grace does *not* say "you got it wrong again"; she says the problem isn't finished.

**[0:04] Warm-up + review**

**Grace:** Board: `5x + 2 = 3x + 10`. Ninety seconds, solo start — show me your first two steps, not the answer.

> **[2] Q1 (L0 probing question at the session level: "first two steps" asks for the attempt, which is the diagnosis).** Solo start protects R4 across four students.

**DeShawn:** I got all the x's on the left: `5x − 3x + 2`… wait, do I move the 2 or the 3x first?

**Grace:** Either works, if the balance holds. Pick one and keep both sides even — then compare with Elena.

> **[3] F1 (specific: "if the balance holds" names the *criterion*, not the step) + R4 (pair work spreads attention).** Grace refuses to choose for him — the strategy choice is the student's.

**[0:08] Guided practice**

**Tomás:** *(on his paper, from `7x − 4 = 3x + 8`)* `7x + 4 = 3x + 8`.

**Grace:** Hold on — before we go further: walk me through what happened to the −4.

> **[4] Q3 (error response: probing question aimed at the exact spot — not "you forgot to flip the sign").** Tomás has moved the term and *added* 4: the M2 sign error when moving terms.

**Tomás:** I… moved it over. So it becomes plus 4?

**Grace:** *(three seconds)* When you move the −4 from the left side, what operation are you actually doing?

> **[5] Q4 (wait time) + L1 (conceptual nudge — points at the *operation*, not the memorized "flip" rule).** The goal: Tomás reconnects "moving" to "subtracting," instead of patching with a remembered flip.

**Tomás:** Subtracting it… from the left side. So the left becomes `7x − 4 − 4`? No wait — if I want it gone, I *subtract* 4 from both sides. So it's minus 4 on both sides, not plus on one.

**Grace:** Say that last part again for the group, slower.

> **[6] F1 + R4 (specific feedback + the student becomes the teacher).** "Say it again for the group" is the strongest form of feedback at this level: his reasoning, broadcast, becomes the review.

**Tomás:** You subtract 4 from *both* sides. So it's `7x − 8 = 3x + 4`. Because the equals sign means both sides stay equal.

**[0:16] Guided practice, continued — Mei hits the wall**

**Mei:** Mine keeps coming out wrong. *(third attempt at `6x + 5 = 2x − 3`; every time, the −3 gives her trouble)* I keep getting positive 3. This is stupid.

> **[7] F2 (distress signal: "this is stupid" — Grace must respond to the feeling *and* the pattern; this is attempt 3 on the same step).** By the ladder's rules this is exactly the worked-example moment: L4, not more hints.

**Grace:** It's not stupid — that −3 is genuinely the sneakiest part of this problem type, and you're in good company. New plan: watch me do one like it, all the way through, and your only job is to catch the moment the −3 gets handled. `6x + 1 = 2x − 7`. First, x's to the left…

> **[8] L4 — worked example after repeated failure (a *similar* problem, same structure, different numbers — never Mei's own problem).** Grace also names what Mei will watch for ("the moment the −3 gets handled") so the example has a target. Emotional reset first ("not stupid… good company"), then a concrete way back in.

**Grace:** …subtract `2x` from both sides — that's `4x + 1 = −7`. Now the tricky guest, the `+1`: subtract 1 from *both* sides — watch — `4x = −8`. Last step, divide by 4: `x = −2`. Check: 6(−2) + 1 = −11, and 2(−2) − 7 = −11. Both sides, same number — the balance held. Mei: which moment was the −3's cousin?

> **[9] L4 execution details worth copying:** every step justified in a few words; the *same* operation that troubled Mei is narrated slowly; ends by handing the insight back with a question, not with a test.

**Mei:** Subtracting the 1 from both sides. So in mine I should subtract the… 5? From both sides.

**Grace:** Your turn — original problem, from the top.

> **[10] L4 hand-back: the worked example *returns* to the student's own problem.** Without this step, worked examples entertain; with it, they transfer.

**Mei:** `6x + 5 = 2x − 3`… minus 2x both sides: `4x + 5 = −3`. Minus 5 both sides: `4x = −8`. x = −2. *(checks)* −11 and −11!

**Grace:** You did the whole ladder yourself. Why subtract the 5 and not add it?

> **[11] F3 (confirm and ask why) — one why-question at the moment of success.** Note Mei *checked her own work* — Grace never touched the pen.

**[0:25] Independent try + exit ticket**

**Grace:** Everyone — one solo problem, then it's the ticket. `8x − 3 = 4x + 5`. Tomás, watch that −3. Ticket's on the table: same family, two questions. I collect them at the door even if they're half-done.

> **[12] F4 (offer a similar problem) + S3 + D1 (exit ticket protected at the end of the hour; the "half-done is fine" rule keeps the data flowing).** Grace's plan shows tomorrow's review already chosen from today's tagged tickets (D3).

## What to steal from T2

- The L4 moment: after ~three failed attempts on the same step, *stop hinting* — reset the emotion, work a similar problem with the trap narrated, hand the original back.
- Wait time held under pressure — annotation [5] — when the easy thing is to correct.
- "Say it again for the group": feedback that converts one student's repair into the group's instruction.
- The ticket habit surviving a messy middle: structure is what protects the data.
