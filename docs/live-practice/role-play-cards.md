# Role-play cards

{: .note }
**For the two [live practice sessions](/tutor-training-course/live-practice/).** All names and profiles are fictional. Student cards are played by adults simulating Grade 7–8 students; each carries (1) a personality note, (2) a misconception profile that drives how they "get stuck," and (3) a reveal rule — the misconception information they give up **only if the tutor's question actually targets it**. The reveal mechanic is what turns role-play into probing-question practice: a tutor who corrects or lectures gets nothing, which is the point.

Print single-sided, cut, one full set per trio. Online: paste each card into its breakout-room chat at round start.

---

## Student cards — Session A (routine rehearsal; cooperative to disruptive)

**S-A1 · Amara, Grade 7 · cooperative**
Wants to please; copies yesterday's method without checking whether it fits. Personality: quiet, second-guesses, waits to be called on. Reveal: will say "is this right?" after every step — the tutor's job is to hand checking back to her.

**S-A2 · Jayden, Grade 7 · fast finisher**
Finishes early, calls out answers to neighbors, gets restless. Reveal: correct answers but zero method — ask him "how do you know?" and he shrugs. His card exists to practice attention-spreading (R4): the tutor must give him a real job without letting him run the room.

**S-A3 · Luis, Grade 7 · avoidant**
Last week said "negative numbers are out to get me." Will start work late, hopes not to be noticed. Reveal: if asked *one specific thing about his own work*, engages genuinely; if lectured, checks out further. Reveal rule: engagement is the success signal.

**S-A4 · Camila, Grade 8 · disruptive-adjacent**
Side conversations, phone under the table, "when's this over." Reveal: has a real misconception (M1) and will work if the tutor gives her the pen and a two-minute solo win. Tests block boundaries, not warmth.

## Student cards — Session B (error handling; reveal mechanics)

**S-B1 · "Desi" · misconception M3 (distributing incorrectly)**
Script: on `3(x + 2) = 12`, writes `3x + 2 = 12` and says "distribute is multiply the first thing, right?"
Reveal (if the question lands on the multiplication: "What did the 3 multiply?"): "…everything inside? So 3 × 2… 6. Oh. `3x + 6`."
Reveal (if corrected instead): shrugs, writes `3x + 6`, learns nothing, will repeat the error next week. Give the tutor nothing else.

**S-B2 · "Mika" · misconception M2 (sign when moving terms)**
Script: on `x − 4 = 10`, writes `x = 6` confidently — "move the 4 over, flip the… wait, minus?"
Reveal (if asked "what did you do to both sides?"): "I subtracted 4 from the left… and the right? I didn't touch the right."
Reveal (if told "check your signs"): guessing — cycles through `x = 14`, `x = 6`. Only a question about the *operation* triggers the real reveal.

**S-B3 · "Theo" · pressure profile · "just tell me"**
Script: reads the problem, writes nothing, says "can you just show me? I learn from examples."
Reveal: if the tutor works a full example *before any attempt*, complies happily and produces nothing — the round is lost and the observer records it. If the tutor requires an attempt first, works reluctantly and then genuinely (he can do it; the ask is avoidance). Reveal rule: after a genuine attempt, accepts ladder hints at one rung per turn.

**S-B4 · "Ren" · distress profile · three failures**
Script: third failed attempt at the same move-the-terms step. "This is stupid. I did the exact same thing. I'm just bad at this."
Reveal: if the tutor keeps hinting, disengages ("sure. whatever.") — the round is lost. If the tutor acknowledges the feeling AND switches to a worked example of a similar problem (L4), re-engages and can name the step on the hand-back. Reveal rule: engagement after the L4 hand-back is the success signal.

**S-B5 · "Priya" · silent student**
Script: writes a wrong first step, then stops. Answers "I don't know" to any question.
Reveal: the written first step shows M4 (divided only the x-term). If the tutor waits 3–5 seconds and asks about *what she wrote* (not "what are you thinking"), she points to the step and recovers. If the tutor fills the silence, stays silent the whole round.

## Tutor cards (both sessions — one per tutor, per round)

**T-A (Session A)** "You have 12 minutes to run all five blocks: warm-up → review → guided practice → independent try → exit ticket. The facilitator calls the 'minutes.' Your plan sheet must show the exit ticket chosen before you start. Success = the ticket is collected, even if everything else wobbles."

**T-B (Session B)** "Your student is stuck on one step. Rules: no explaining until you've asked; one ladder rung per turn (hand on the card); 3–5 seconds of silence after each question; never name the misconception; never perform the fix. Success = the student finds it by L3 without you touching the pen."

## Observer cards (both sessions)

**O-A (Session A)** "You have the timing sheet and the [Domain 1+2 checklist](session-a.md#observer-checklist-domain-2--domain-1--print-per-round). Call 'minute 5, 10, 15…' out loud. Record actual block timings. In the debrief, report timings FIRST, impressions second — data before vibes."

**O-B (Session B)** "You have the [Domain 3+4 checklist](session-b.md#observer-checklist-domain-3--domain-4--print-per-round). Two extra jobs: (1) count the silence — fingers up, one per second, after each tutor question; (2) call the rung number when the tutor's hand moves on the ladder card. Report the rung sequence in the debrief: L0 → L1 → hand-back is the target shape."

## Card mechanics for facilitators

1. **One card per student per round** — profiles stack badly and overloading the tutor trains panic, not skill.
2. Escalate across rounds: Session A goes cooperative → disruptive; Session B goes plain error → pressure → distress.
3. The reveal rules are the assessment instrument. If a round "fails" because the tutor lectured, that IS the round's result — record it, re-run one exchange with a better question, move on.
4. Debrief the students' experience, not the tutor's intention: "What did you notice when the question landed?" teaches more than "You should have waited longer."
5. Keep the fiction respectful: students are written as capable kids with real, research-documented misconceptions — never as comic relief. The [misconception bank](/tutor-training-course/tools/exit-ticket-bank.md) tags every card's misconception so tutors can connect role-play to real tickets later.
