# Exit-ticket bank

{: .label .label-green }
20 items · Grade 7–8 linear equations · Every item is tagged by **skill** and **misconception** (M1–M5, the same taxonomy as the [Socratic AI Tutor](https://iwyusdaz.github.io/socratic-ai-tutor/)) · Each item includes the **probing question** the tutor reaches for when it's missed — the ticket tells you *where to dig*; the question finds out *why*.

{: .note }
Items are drawn at 1–2 per session from the bank, chosen **before** the session ([session plan template](session-plan-template.md)). Tags: **SOLVE** two-step solve · **DIST** distributing · **MOVE** moving terms · **DIV** dividing · **BAL** balance meaning of the equals sign · **M1–M5** misconception most likely to surface.

---

## Tier 1 — solve `ax + b = c` (weeks 1–2)

**Item 1 · `3x + 5 = 20` · SOLVE · M1 risk**
The classic. Miss → probe: *"You wrote 3x = 20. When you subtracted 5, what did you subtract from the right side?"* (M1: operation applied to one side only.)

**Item 2 · `2x − 7 = 9` · SOLVE · M2 risk**
Negative constant. Miss → probe: *"The −7 moved across the equals sign. What operation is 'moving' it, and what happened on the right side?"* (M2.)

**Item 3 · `x + 4 = 7` · SOLVE · M5 risk**
Deliberately trivial — and the sharpest M5 detector there is. A student who writes `x = 7` (reading `=` as "the answer comes next") or `x + 7` exposes the operational equals sign. Miss → probe: *"When you wrote the 7 after the x, what were you saying the whole line meant? What does the = promise about the two sides?"*

**Item 4 · `12 = 4x − 8` · SOLVE · M1 risk**
Reversed form (constant first) — students trained on `ax + b = c` sometimes "can't start." Miss → probe: *"What's the same about this one and `4x − 8 = 12`? What does the = let you do?"* (Tests whether the balance idea, not a left-to-right recipe, is doing the work.)

**Item 5 · `6x + 2 = 2x + 22` · SOLVE/MOVE · M2 risk**
First x-terms-on-both-sides item. Miss → probe: *"You moved the 2x — subtract or add, and from which sides?"*

## Tier 2 — distribute then solve (weeks 2–3)

**Item 6 · `2(x + 3) = 10` · DIST · M3 risk**
Miss → probe: *"What did the 2 multiply — say all of it out loud."*

**Item 7 · `3(x − 2) = 9` · DIST · M3 + M2 risk**
Negative inside. Miss → probe: *"What is 3 × −2? Where does that land on the right side?"*

**Item 8 · `2(x − 4) = 2x − 4` · DIST · M3 (classic)**
True/false format: "Amara wrote `2x − 4`. Is she right? Why or why not?" Miss → probe: *"What did the 2 multiply in her line?"* (Explaining someone else's error lowers the stakes of being wrong — keep this format for recurring errors.)

**Item 9 · `4(x + 1) − 2 = 14` · DIST + SOLVE · M3 risk**
Two moves before the solve. Miss → probe: *"Read your second line. What happened to the −2 — which terms did it hit?"* (Distinguishes M3 recurrence from M1.)

**Item 10 · `5(x + 2) = 5x + 10` · DIST/BAL · M3 + M5 risk**
Identity format — "is this true for every x? How do you know?" Tests whether "both terms" was learned as a rule or as a meaning. Miss → probe: *"Pick a number for x. Test both sides. What does that tell you about the equation?"*

## Tier 3 — variables on both sides (weeks 3–5)

**Item 11 · `5x + 3 = 3x + 11` · MOVE · M1/M2 risk**
Miss → probe: *"When you subtracted 3x, which sides changed? What's left on each?"*

**Item 12 · `3(x − 2) + 1 = 13` · DIST + MOVE · M3 risk**
The item used in the worked [session plan](session-plan-template.md). Miss → probe: *"What did the 3 multiply? Then walk me through what you moved."*

**Item 13 · `7x − 4 = 4x + 8` · MOVE · M2 risk**
The moving coefficient. Miss → probe: *"The 4x moved from the right. What operation removed it — and what did that do to the left side?"*

**Item 14 · `9x + 4 = 4x + 4` · MOVE · M5 risk**
Solution `x = 0` — the zero answer spooks students ("is that allowed?"). Miss → probe: *"Test x = 0 in both sides. What do they give you? What does the = say about that?"* (Converts the scare into the balance meaning.)

**Item 15 · `6x − 3 = 2x + 5` → a student writes `6x − 3 = 5` after "moving 2x" · MOVE · M1**
Error shown on the item (true/false again). Probe: *"When 2x left the right side, what should have happened on the left? What does = promise?"*

## Tier 4 — dividing and special cases (weeks 5–8)

**Item 16 · `4x = 12` → a student writes `x = 12` · DIV · M4 (classic)**
Shown as an error to explain. Probe: *"You divided the 4x by 4 — what did you divide on the other side of the equals sign?"*

**Item 17 · `4x + 2 = 14` → a student writes `x + 2 = 3.5`… then `x = 1.5` · DIV · M4**
The "divided everything except the +2" error. Probe: *"If you divide ONE side by 4, what has to happen to the other side — every term on it?"*

**Item 18 · `2x = 2(x + 3) − 6` · BAL · M5 risk**
True for every x after simplifying — the identity case. Probe: *"Simplify the right side. What's left? Pick any x — test it. What kind of equation is true for ALL x?"*

**Item 19 · `3x + 1 = 3x + 5` · BAL · M5 risk**
No solution — another equals-sign-concept probe. Probe: *"Simplify. What's left on each side? Can any x make 1 = 5? What do we call an equation with no solution?"*

**Item 20 · `−2x + 6 = −2(x − 3)` · BAL · M5 + M3 risk**
Identity in disguise; the −2 distribution is the trap. Probe: *"Distribute carefully, term by term. What does the right side become? Test x = 10 to be sure."*

## Using the bank

1. **Choose before the session** (template rule). One item the *group* should mostly get (confidence), one at the edge (information).
2. **Record with tags, not just scores** — the [tracker](student-progress-tracker.md) wants the tag (`12·DIST`), because "missed item 12" is trivia while "missed DIST twice" is a plan.
3. **Escalation trigger:** two consecutive tickets below mastery on the same tag → coordinator flag ([D4](https://github.com/iwyusdaz/tutor-training-course/blob/main/research/competency-model.md)). The tags make that automatic.
4. **The probing question column is the bridge to practice:** when an item is missed, the *next* session's guided practice opens with its probe. Ticket → probe → plan is the whole [data loop](../modules/5-using-exit-tickets-and-data.md).
5. Items 3, 10, 14, 18, 19 are **concept items** (BAL/M5): use them when tickets say solves are fine but transfers keep failing — procedural success with conceptual collapse is exactly what these five are built to catch.
