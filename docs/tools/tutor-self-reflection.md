# Tutor self-reflection form

{: .label .label-green }
Weekly, ~10 minutes · Feeds the [coaching cycle](/tutor-training-course/coaching/coaching-cycle.md) — your coach reads this *before* the debrief, so the conversation starts where you already are.

{: .note }
**Nothing here is graded or submitted anywhere.** This form is for you (and your coach, if you choose to share it). Honest is more useful than impressive — "my tickets went unrecorded twice" is a coaching conversation; "everything went fine" is a dead end.

---

## The five prompts (one per competency domain)

**1 · Relationships** — *Which student did I address least this week, and what did I miss as a result?*
(If the answer is "they were fine," look at the tracker: who has no flag, no note, no name-reference in your plans? Invisible is not fine.)

**2 · Session structure** — *Which block did I steal from, and what did the stolen minutes buy?*
(Stealing from the exit ticket buys nothing but the data gap. Stealing from guided practice usually buys a session that felt productive.)

**3 · Questions and errors** — *When did I last answer my own question? What was I afraid the silence would mean?*
(Then: one probing question I used this week that actually landed — what made it land?)

**4 · Feedback and motivation** — *Whose success did I praise, and what exactly did I name?*
(If the answer is "good job, mostly," pick one student for next week and write the specific sentence in advance.)

**5 · Data use** — *What did this week's tickets change in my next plans? If nothing — why am I collecting them?*

## The one-line habit

End by filling this sentence and keeping it where you'll see it at your next session:

> **"Next session, the one thing I'm rehearsing is: ____________________."**

One thing. The [coaching cycle](/tutor-training-course/coaching/coaching-cycle.md) works because it concentrates on a single focus move per observation — this line is your version of the same discipline. Bring it to every observation debrief.

## For your coach

If you share this form (recommended, not required), your coach will:

- read it **before** the observation debrief,
- open the conversation with your strength first ([conversation guide](/tutor-training-course/coaching/coaching-conversation-guide.md), section 2),
- pick the **single** focus move with you — which is usually already visible in your own "one thing I'm rehearsing" line,
- never quote your reflection in program metrics. Reflection is for learning; the [measurement plan](/tutor-training-course/program/measurement-plan.md) runs on observation and ticket data, not on what you worried about at 10 pm.

## A worked example (fictional)

> Diego's reflection, week 3 (abridged): *"Least addressed: Camila — she works so fast I forget her. Structure: guided practice again ate the hour. Questions: I answered my own question on the second problem — she'd barely read it. Feedback: praised Aaliyah's speed, which I regret. Data: tickets changed nothing Tuesday because I forgot to bring them. Next session, rehearsing: the 'two minutes left — land this one' sentence, and ONE probing question written on the plan before I walk in."*
>
> His coach's debrief opens with the Camila win ("you noticed the fastest student needs a job — that's real insight"), names the strength, and picks ONE focus move: the pre-written probing question. The hour-theft and the ticket lapse come back in Week 4, not Week 3. One focus at a time is why the cycle works.
