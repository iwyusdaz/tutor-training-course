# Module 2 · The 30-minute session

{: .label .label-green }
About 25 minutes · Competency domain: **Session structure** · [competency model](/tutor-training-course/research/competency-model.md)

## Objectives

By the end of this module you can:
1. **Run the five-part 30-minute routine within its time boxes** — warm-up (5'), review (5'), guided practice (12'), independent try (5'), exit ticket (3') — behaviors S1–S2 in the [competency model](/tutor-training-course/research/competency-model.md).
2. **End every session with the exit ticket administered and collected** (S3) — the single most-skipped behavior, and the one the program's data loop depends on ([rubric Domain 2](/tutor-training-course/coaching/observation-rubric/)).

## Scenario hook: the session that ran away

> Grace, 52, a former accountant, is detail-oriented and loved by her students. Her session plan says 30 minutes. Her actual sessions run 40 — every time. Guided practice absorbs everything: she walks through each problem completely, step by step, and when the clock hits the exit-ticket block she says "we'll do it next time."
>
> "Next time" never comes. Three weeks in, her coordinator can't tell whether Grace's students are learning, because Grace has *no per-student data* — and Grace herself can't remember who struggled with what.

Grace's problem is not effort or warmth; it's that without a protected structure, quality work expands until it eats the routine. The routine is what makes tutoring *consistent* — and consistency is one of the few things programs can actually manage at scale.

## Key idea: five parts, one purpose each

| Block | Minutes | Purpose | What an observer sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Warm-up | 5 | Reactivate last session, settle the group | Students working within 2 minutes; same-skill problem, no new teaching |
| 2 · Review | 5 | Read last exit ticket *with* the group | Students self-correct yesterday's common error; tutor names the trap |
| 3 · Guided practice | 12 | New skill, with you inside their thinking | You ask; they step. Hint ladder one rung per turn |
| 4 · Independent try | 5 | Prove the fix transfers | Silence from you; students work alone on a similar problem |
| 5 · Exit ticket | 3 | Close the data loop | One item per student, collected even if incomplete |

Two structural notes:

- **The exit ticket is not optional.** In the [State of Tutoring 2026 analysis](https://poweredbypearl.com/tutoring-nears-research-benchmark-after-esser-but-averages-hide-big-differences-among-students-analysis-of-nearly-900000-sessions-finds/), the median student got about **72 minutes a week** against a research benchmark of roughly **90 in three 30-minute sessions** — and under-delivery varied enormously *within* programs. Session-by-session data is how a coordinator finds the students quietly receiving 23 minutes instead of 90. Skipping the ticket blinds the program.
- **Timers beat discipline.** Run a visible countdown on your phone or the board. When guided practice is over, it is over — the independent try is where students prove they don't need you.

## Model: protecting the time boxes

Excerpt from [Transcript T2](/tutor-training-course/transcripts/t2/) (**fictional, constructed for training**), minute 22 of 30:

> **Tutor:** Two minutes left of practice, so let's land this one. Mei, read us where you got to.
>
> **Mei:** I have `3(x + 2) − 4 = 11`. I distributed and got `3x + 2 − 4`.
>
> **Tutor:** Hold that thought — that's exactly our review item tomorrow. [keeps the error alive, defers it: the routine, not the rabbit hole] Independent try: everyone solves `2(x + 5) = 26` alone. Five minutes. If you finish, check by substituting back. [starts the timer]
>
> *(At minute 30, tickets are out. Two students hand in complete answers; Jayden's is half-finished — it gets collected anyway.)* [S3 even when imperfect]

**What to notice:** the tutor names Mei's error, declines to fix it *inside the wrong block*, and converts it into tomorrow's review. Every block ends on time because the tutor treats the plan as a contract with the data loop.

## Practice with feedback

**Your turn:** Below is a real-shaped session log from a weak session. Diagnose which blocks were stolen, and write the two changes you would make.

> 0–2' Students trickle in; tutor hands out worksheets. · 2–21' Tutor explains solving `4x + 2 = 14` completely at the board, then two more examples. · 21–26' Students start problems; tutor hovers. · 26–30' "Great work today! Same time Thursday." No exit ticket.

{: .feedback }
**Diagnosis:** warm-up (missing — cold start), review (missing), guided practice ran 19 minutes (should be 12), independent try shrank to hover-time, exit ticket (missing — the data loop is broken). **Fixes:** (1) protect blocks with a visible timer and start warm-up at minute zero as students arrive; (2) plan the exit ticket *into* the plan, not after it — pick it from the [exit-ticket bank](/tutor-training-course/tools/exit-ticket-bank/) when writing the session plan, so it can't be dropped. A tutor who over-explains is usually practicing the wrong block: move your explanation into the 5-minute review, where it is scheduled.

## In your next session, try…

1. Fill the [session plan template](/tutor-training-course/tools/session-plan-template/) *including* the chosen exit ticket before the session.
2. Run one full session with a visible countdown for each block.
3. Collect the exit ticket from every student, finished or not, and note completion on your tracker.

## Reflection

In the [tutor self-reflection form](/tutor-training-course/tools/tutor-self-reflection/): *Which block do I steal from, and what does that block give my students that I didn't want to lose?* (If your answer is "guided practice" — so does everyone's. The fix is planning less content, not moving faster.)

## Check yourself

- You are 4 minutes behind at the end of guided practice. What happens to the exit ticket? *(It still happens — cut guided practice next session, not the data.)*
- Why "independent try" instead of more guided practice? *(Transfer: the point of tutoring is that students can do it without you. Five silent minutes is the cheapest honest test.)*
