# Simulated dashboard · Week 6

{: .label .label-yellow }
**All data on this page is SIMULATED and fictional** — Bridge Math Tutoring and its six schools do not exist. The dashboard exists to demonstrate *how a program manager reads fidelity data*: find the seam, name the interpretation, commit to an action, set a check date.

## The six schools (week 6 of the semester)

| School | Tutors | Avg min/student/wk | Students <60 min | Same-tutor % | Sessions w/ ticket | Mean rubric (1–4) | Flags open |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lakeside MS | 12 | 88 | 2 | 92% | 94% | 3.2 | 3 |
| Harborview MS | 10 | 91 | 1 | 88% | 96% | 3.4 | 2 |
| Cedar Grove MS | 9 | 84 | 3 | 84% | 91% | 3.0 | 4 |
| Maple Ridge MS | 11 | **58** | **14** | **71%** | 88% | 2.9 | 9 |
| Summit MS | 8 | 86 | 2 | 90% | 89% | **2.4** | 5 |
| Riverbend MS | 10 | 83 | 4 | 87% | 93% | 3.1 | 4 |

**Reading rules for this table:** dosage benchmark is ~90 min/week (flag <60); same-tutor target ≥85%; ticket target ≥90%; rubric target mean ≥3.0 with no domain at 1. [Measurement plan](measurement-plan.md) carries the definitions and sources.

## The under-delivering school: Maple Ridge

**What the row says:** 58 min/student/week against a ~90 benchmark; **14 of ~120 students below 60 minutes**; same-tutor continuity 71% (target 85); ticket rate 88% (just under target); rubric 2.9 — quality *within* sessions is near standard. Flags: 9, concentrated in MOVE·M2 and DIST·M3.

**What it is NOT saying:** Maple Ridge's tutors are not weak — the rubric row is fine. This is a *delivery* problem: students are scheduled or pulled inconsistently, tutors are being reshuffled (71% continuity), and the ticket rate is drifting. The 9 flags are the downstream symptom: fragmented dosage produces exactly the misconception recurrence the tags are catching.

**Interpretation (the story the data tells):** scheduling debt. When the school's master schedule loses rooms or test weeks eat pull-out slots, sessions get "made up" by whichever tutor is free — dosage fragments, continuity drops, and each student's remaining minutes lose the compounding of a consistent adult who knows their last error.

**Action plan (owned by the site coordinator, with the school's scheduling lead):**

| Action | Owner | By when |
|---|---|---|
| Pull the attendance log for the 14 under-dosage students; identify *where* the minutes die (Mondays? test weeks?) | Coordinator | Week 7 |
| Lock the 3×30 grid: every student has named slots with a named tutor; print it into the [session plan](../tools/session-plan-template.md) headers | Coordinator + scheduling lead | Week 7 |
| Stop tutor reassignment for the flagged 14: continuity first for the students already below 60 minutes | Scheduling lead | Week 7 |
| Ticket-rate patch: exit tickets move to the end of *every* session with a printed "even if incomplete" rule re-briefed at the next site meeting ([T5]'s fix) | Coordinator | Week 8 |
| Re-check | — | **Week 9: target ≥75 min avg, <8 students below 60, continuity ≥85%** |

If week 9 shows the same row: escalate to the program director — the constraint is probably rooms or staffing, which scheduling goodwill cannot fix, and the [measurement plan](measurement-plan.md)'s escalation path (program-level, week 9) exists for exactly that.

## The other five, quickly

- **Summit MS — the quality seam.** Dosage fine (86), ticket rate fine, **rubric 2.4**. This is the opposite failure: the routine runs, the teaching inside it is generic (a rubric that low usually means Domain 3 — corrections instead of probing questions, [T6] patterns). Action: coaching cycles concentrated there, [calibration re-check](../coaching/calibration-activity.md) first (rule out a severe scorer before concluding a weak site), [Session B](../live-practice/session-b.md) materials in the next site meeting.
- **Harborview MS — the healthy control.** 91 minutes, 96% tickets, 3.4 rubric. Its value is comparative: what "working" looks like in this program's own numbers (and a check on scorer drift — is Harborview's observer generous or is the site genuinely strong? The [calibration log](../coaching/calibration-activity.md) answers).
- **Lakeside MS — watch the flags.** Good averages, 3 flags on BAL·M5 — all from one tutor's group. One-group clusters mean a teaching-level misconception (the balance idea explained procedurally), not a program problem. Coaching-cycle work, [exit-ticket bank](../tools/exit-ticket-bank.md) items 3/14/18/19 as the re-teach set.
- **Cedar Grove MS — mid-pack, stable.** 84 minutes with 3 low students is a normal week-6 shape; the action is the per-student check, not a program intervention.
- **Riverbend MS — the quiet one to watch.** 83 minutes, 93% tickets, 3.1 rubric, but 4 flags and the same-tutor % is drifting (87 → check next month). Nothing to do today except notice it — which is precisely what leading indicators are for.

## What a simulated dashboard can and cannot show

- **CAN:** demonstrate the reading discipline — find the seam, separate delivery from quality, act on the leading indicators, set a re-check date; show how the metric table becomes decisions.
- **CANNOT:** claim that any real school, program, or tutor behaves like this. Every number above is invented to be *instructive*, not representative. No real children, schools, or programs are depicted anywhere in this portfolio.
